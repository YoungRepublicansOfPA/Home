What changed under Governor Josh Shapiro?

August 4, 2023

Property tax and rent rebates

Shapiro signed an expansion that raised the maximum standard rebate from $650 to $1,000. It set the income limit at $45,000 for homeowners and renters and indexed the limit to inflation.

Read the signing announcement
July 12, 2024

School funding formula

The enacted 2024–25 budget directed nearly $526 million through a new formula based on the gap between a district’s resources and its funding needs.

Read the state’s school funding breakdown
November 12, 2025

Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit

The enacted budget created a state credit equal to 10% of the federal earned income tax credit. The administration projected $193 million in first-year relief. The amount ultimately claimed depends on tax filings.

Read the budget announcement
July 12, 2026

Child care and vocational education

Shapiro signed the 2026–27 budget with funding for child care and vocational education. The fiscal year runs through June 30, 2027. Final spending figures follow fiscal close.

1925 fiscal benchmark

The Census reported approximately $111.18 million in Pennsylvania state revenue receipts, including about $91.34 million in taxes. Governmental-cost payments were approximately $111.6 million, including $34.52 million in outlays. Figures are rounded nominal dollars under the 1925 classification.

Social Security and the Pennsylvania paycheck

In 2026 an employee earning $50,000 in covered wages pays $3,100 in federal Social Security payroll tax. The employer contributes another $3,100. The employee and employer rates are each 6.2% on covered wages up to $184,500. Self-employed workers generally pay a combined 12.4% on covered net earnings up to that ceiling.

These contributions finance federal retirement, survivor and disability benefits. Changing the federal rate requires federal legislation. A Pennsylvania governor can advocate for it and ask the state’s congressional delegation to sponsor a bill. Pennsylvania already excludes Social Security benefit payments from state personal income tax.

Our policy position. We want Governor Shapiro to advocate for ending the federal Social Security payroll tax. Readers can evaluate a proposal by looking for its bill text, projected change in take-home pay, and how it would fund promised benefits.
