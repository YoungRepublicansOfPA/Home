Historical public benefit spending

How much public money supports Pennsylvania residents, and where does it come from? This first series follows Medicaid. The statewide total across all benefit programs remains unverified. Adding agency budgets to the programs they fund would count some dollars twice.

Statewide, the PHFA roster lists 399 project awards totaling $64,075,000 for 2025/2026. Select a county to see its projects and named recipients. Shared awards retain their full service area. Township names appear when identified in the project record. Combined spending across all programs and townships remains undetermined.

The historical LIHEAP report lists $78,081,661 in statewide cash assistance and $29,833,701 in crisis assistance. The extracted county cash figures sum to $20,000 more than the statewide table.

Who receives housing awards?

PHFA’s 2025/2026 PHARE roster names Armstrong Habitat for Humanity ($100,000 for critical home repairs), the Urban League of Philadelphia ($250,000 for housing for returning citizens), and Abington Township ($200,000 for emergency repairs in Montgomery County). KenCrest Services received a $100,000 award covering four counties. Its published amount applies across the service area.

These awards show who has funding to carry out a project. The payment schedule, eligible expenses and reimbursement terms explain what the grantee has a financial incentive to deliver. Actual disbursements and project results require additional records.

Assistance outside the Department of Human Services

Federal earned income tax credit

The IRS reports approximately 826,600 Pennsylvania claims totaling $2.3 billion for tax year 2024 among returns processed in 2025. This federal credit includes amounts applied against income tax and refundable amounts. The IRS table combines both. IRS state tables

Pennsylvania property tax and rent rebates

For 2024 claims, the Department of Revenue reports $185.0 million in homeowner rebates and $130.0 million in renter rebates. Together these categories account for $315.0 million. The report traces the program to 1971. Pennsylvania’s budget office documents transfers from the Property Tax Relief Fund to the Lottery Fund for rebate claims.
