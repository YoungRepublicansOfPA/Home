Pennsylvania mortality and abortion statistics

In 2024, Pennsylvania recorded 135,987 resident deaths, including 1,812 deaths by suicide and 674 by homicide. A county count can show the scale of a loss. To decide where prevention resources could help, start with the ages affected and compare rates among populations of similar size and age.

Drug use and overdoses

Every life has value. For families, churches and schools looking to prevent another loss, a starting point is understanding who reports using substances and where people are dying.

3,341 fatal drug overdoses in 2024

The Pennsylvania Department of Health reported a 29.2% decline from 2023 across all ages. Just over half (54.5%) of deaths occurred at the person’s own home. The report maps counts and rates by county and includes nonfatal overdose indicators. Cause and intent categories can overlap: adding this count to suicides or all-cause deaths would double-count some people. Pennsylvania overdose data brief and county map

Which birth years does an age group cover?

A named generation can cut across several reported age bands. For example, people aged 15–24 during 2024 could have been born from 1999 through 2009, depending on their birthday and the event date. The tables above cannot tell us exactly how many belonged to each birth year.

From the records to local prevention

More than half of Pennsylvania’s 2024 fatal overdoses occurred in the person’s own home. Families and congregations can check where someone nearby could obtain naloxone, learn to use it and find ongoing support. The state offers training and access to free supplies for individuals and organizations. Get naloxone and training.

For school prevention planning, compare your county’s survey with the statewide results for the same grade, survey year and recall period. Ask which students took part and which protective factors the survey measured. Read the county youth survey reports.

Follow the public dollars

If a county funds overdose prevention, find the appropriation in its adopted budget and the decision in the meeting minutes. Ask who received the money, how many residents the program reached and when the county will publish results. Compare several years of resident death rates alongside those records. A spending increase or a single year’s change in deaths cannot establish the program’s effect.
