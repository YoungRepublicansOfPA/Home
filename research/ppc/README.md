# Pennsylvania Partnerships for Children
Independent research profile, reviewed November 24, 2026. Inclusion identifies an information resource. The project has no announced partnership with PPC.

## History and work
Lucy Durr Hackney founded this statewide policy advocacy nonprofit in the early 1990s. PPC has led Pennsylvania’s KIDS COUNT work for more than 30 years. Its reports, data maps, legislative advocacy and coalition organizing cover child welfare, early learning, home visiting, schools, and maternal and child health. [PPC history](https://www.papartnerships.org/who-we-are/about-ppc/)

## What its proposals would do
The [December 2024 summary](roadmap-summary.pdf) sets priorities. The [full 2025–26 roadmap](roadmap.pdf) gives more detail. Both record what PPC wanted at the time. Current law and enacted appropriations require separate checks.

| Proposal | Mechanism and fiscal question |
|---|---|
| Expand public pre-K and subsidized child care | Public funds pay providers for eligible children. How many additional staffed places would a specific appropriation buy, at what annual cost? |
| Expand voluntary home visiting | PPC seeks state appropriations and state matching funds to draw federal MIECHV dollars. What state match is required and what costs persist after the federal award ends? |
| Expand technical education | PPC calls for a larger state CTE subsidy and equipment grants. Separate recurring instructor costs from equipment purchases. Measure credentials earned and job placement. |
| State earned income tax credit | Targeted tax relief changes net resources across households. The net budget cost depends on credit design, eligibility and claims. |
| Support relatives caring for foster children | PPC proposes changes to kinship policy and practice. Measure placement stability, safety and costs against other placements. |

Several proposals explicitly allocate public resources to particular households, schools or providers. The December roadmap does not give a single package price, a proposed tax rate, a complete list of offsets or a quantified forecast of child outcomes. A spending request can be funded from existing revenue, an offset or new revenue. The roadmap alone does not establish which taxpayer would owe more.

## Evidence about children
PPC credits coalition work on Pennsylvania’s CHIP law in its [historical timeline](history.pdf). The [state’s 2024 CHIP report](chip-2024.pdf) confirms the 1992 law and reports about 167,000 enrolled children and teens in December 2024. Enrollment measures insurance coverage. It cannot tell us how many children gained coverage specifically because of PPC.

PPC advocates for Pre-K Counts. A [UNC evaluation](pre-k-evaluation.pdf) found participating children performed better on kindergarten vocabulary and math than comparison children without center-based early education. The study was observational and did not find significant differences across all measured domains. It evaluated the preschool program. The reviewed records give no estimate of the incremental child outcome per dollar spent on PPC advocacy.

## Where the 2025 expenses went

Form 990 Part IX reports $590,105 of officer/key employee compensation and $1,471,672 of other salaries and wages. Domestic organizational grants were $1,390,317. Other nonemployee services (line 11g) were $257,531. The remaining $328,896 includes accounting, occupancy, travel and other operating expenses. These categories reconcile to $4,038,521. Schedule C identifies $123,771 of direct lobbying within reported expenditures. Part VIII line 1e is blank; PPC classified $3,873,262 as other contributions on line 1f. Part VII reports zero compensation for bank-affiliated directors David Patti and Ellen Kyzer. Schedule I names grants including $331,257 to PennAEYC, $235,382 to Children First and $211,367 to Maternity Care Coalition for campaigns. See the archived [return](2025-IRS990.html.txt), [grants](2025-IRS990ScheduleI.html.txt) and [lobbying](2025-IRS990ScheduleC.html.txt). These are reported expenditures; the return does not independently evaluate campaign results.

## Downloading the historical evidence

[Download the research archive](ppc-research.zip) for the funding timeline, annual financial records, campaign analysis, public reports, chart data and source manifest. Start with funding-history.csv for nominal revenue, expenses and available contributions by fiscal year. Each row retains a source URL. The source manifest supplies SHA-256 hashes for downloaded records. The main return, its schedules and filing summaries have different levels of detail. A donor-by-donor history cannot be inferred from total contributions. Annual amounts must not be added to announced multiyear award amounts without checking the recognition period.
